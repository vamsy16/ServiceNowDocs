1. What ServiceNow Impact is

A premium customer success product — not a normal application

  • Impact is a subscription-based customer success product that combines AI-powered tools with hands-on ServiceNow expertise.
  • It is delivered in three parts:
  • A digital workspace inside our own instance — the Impact Store Application
  • A ServiceNow-hosted expert space — the Impact Delivery Instance (IDI)
  • Human expertise and service entitlements — the Impact Squad, Developer Support, learning credits and Accelerators
  • Stated core functions: plan implementation, track product adoption, maintain platform health, resolve platform issues, access expert support.
  • From Yokohama onward the Impact Store Application is the exclusive hub for new Impact features — the IDI does not receive new features.
  • It does not replace platform administration, change control or Now Support case handling.

2. Why ServiceNow introduced it

Five market drivers behind the product

  • The platform outgrew ticket-based support. Customers stopped asking only “something is broken, fix it” and started asking “are we using what we bought?”
  • Value realisation became a board-level question — finance wants ROI evidence, not anecdotes.
  • Technical debt accumulated silently — leading practices were never measured continuously.
  • Expert capacity is scarce — advice had to be packaged as fixed-scope delivery, not documents.
  • Upgrade fatigue is real — the causes of broken upgrades needed to be attacked before the upgrade window.
  • The product response is a closed loop: instrument → interpret → recommend → measure → repeat.

Platform Health — insight, prevention, remediation

Thousands of leading-practice checks, run continuously

  • HealthScan definitions: the checks. Scan Engine: the runner. Findings: the output — and they stay in our instance.
  • Persona dashboards mean each role sees what it owns: executive, platform owner, team lead, development team.
  • Custom definitions let us encode our own standards; definition suites group them; exception reasons give an approved escape hatch.
  • Real-Time Prevention validates changes as they are made rather than after the fact.
  • Integration: findings can be routed into Jira, Azure DevOps, SPM or CWM so remediation lands in the team's existing backlog.
  • AI: batch remediation with AI proposes fixes for review (needs Now Assist for Impact 3.03+, Now Assist for Platform 11.01+, the skill activated and sn_impact_gen_ai.ai_fix.enabled = true).
  • First full scan can take hours — ServiceNow recommends running it overnight, especially in production.

Instance Observer — performance, availability, user experience

Monitoring from outside the instance — including when the instance is unwell

  • Off-instance observability: if the instance degrades, its own monitoring is often the first casualty — Observer keeps working.
  • Coverage includes core performance, database, ECC queue, email, events, host health, jobs, load balancer, node health, replicas, schedulers and semaphores.
  • Alerting: popular and custom alerts, anomaly-based thresholds, RCC alerts and long-pending-job alert cards, with webhook notifications into our existing channels.
  • Triage and root-cause correlation turn an alert into an investigation with evidence attached.
  • User-experience insights require an admin to accept Terms & Conditions via Manage Permissions; user IDs are anonymised by default with an opt-in for non-anonymised collection.
  • Entitlement-tiered: seats, look-back window, alert counts and analytics differ between Guided and Total — verify. Not available in the mobile app.

8. Prerequisites — readiness before we start

Most stalled deployments stall here, not on technology

  • Platform: Yokohama or Xanadu Patch 2 and above; Impact Store Application entitlement (sn_entitlement) version 4.1.2 or higher — verify the live Store listing.
  • Commercial: confirm the package (Guided / Total / Integrated Success) and any Add-ons; entitlements differ materially.
  • People: a named contact administrator to receive the registration email, a Platform Owner, an Impact App Admin and a Scan Engine Admin.
  • Access: Impact Squad access approval (read-only, 30 days, revocable, quarterly re-approval).
  • Consent: Terms & Conditions acceptance for user-experience monitoring; consent when scheduling the Monthly Health Assessment.
  • Security: dedicated integration user, authentication method chosen (OAuth 2.0 preferred over Basic), data-egress review of Service Bridge, Cloud Storage, Licensing Engine and MIF.
  • Data: exact instance names matching the instance_name property, full URLs, one Production designation.
  • Capacity: storage growth (attachments dominate) and a cutover window for the first full scan.
  • Known limitation: Impact does not support domain separation; portions are unavailable in restricted environments including Australia IRAP-Protected.

9. The first 90 days — Foundations

From activation to the first credible read-out

  • Week 0 — kickoff and alignment: Impact Kickoff Meeting, success criteria agreed, named contacts confirmed, squad access approved.
  • Weeks 1–2 — technical enablement: app installed, Guided Setup started, users and roles assigned, Platform Health users placed in groups, development teams created.
  • Weeks 2–3 — baseline the platform: Scan Engine activated, first full scan run overnight, findings triaged, remediation backlog created.
  • Weeks 2–4 — connect and migrate: instances registered, authentication configured, connection validated, registration completed, Active replication confirmed, data migration run.
  • Weeks 3–6 — agree the value story: objectives and outcomes agreed with baselines and targets; data collection jobs enabled per product.
  • Weeks 4–8 — plan the work: Capability Map reviewed, Product Adoption Roadmap published, recommendations converted into governed work items.
  • Weeks 6–10 — extend observability: Instance Observer enabled, thresholds tuned, webhook notifications routed, consent captured if in scope.
  • Weeks 8–12 — first value read-out: Monthly Health Assessment reviewed, outcome trends analysed, learning credits allocated.
  • Exit criteria: connection verified, full scan complete with owned findings, outcomes with baselines, published roadmap, calendarised reviews.

Slide 4 — 1. What ServiceNow Impact is

Define it precisely, because “Impact” is often heard as “support” or “another module”.

It is a subscription product with three delivery surfaces. The one that matters most for day-to-day work is the Impact Store Application inside our instance — reached at All → Impact. The IDI is where our named squad works; we reach it through the Activity Center. Human expertise is the third piece: CSM, and in higher packages a Customer Success Executive, Platform Architect and Support Account Manager.

The directional statement worth emphasising: from Yokohama onward, new innovative Impact features land in the Store Application, not the IDI. So the app is where our future capability will accumulate.

Close with the boundary: this is not a replacement for our admins or our support contract, and we should not describe it that way to the business.

Slide 11 — Platform Health — insight, prevention, remediation

Slide 12 — Instance Observer — performance, availability, user experience

Position this as the answer to the most demoralising class of incident: performance problems reported by users after the fact, with no evidence left to investigate.

The off-instance argument is the headline: when an instance is degraded, in-instance monitoring is unreliable. Observer watches from outside.

For the operations audience, the practical wins are anomaly-based alerting (fewer noisy static thresholds) and webhook routing into the existing on-call tooling rather than a new console nobody watches.

Raise the consent point deliberately: user-experience monitoring requires Terms & Conditions acceptance by an admin in Manage Permissions, IDs are anonymised by default, and there is an opt-in for non-anonymised collection. That is a conversation for our privacy and employee-representation stakeholders, not a technical toggle.

Finally, flag the entitlement differences and the mobile-app gap so expectations are set correctly.

Slide 13 — Value management — proving ROI, not asserting it

This is the pillar that justifies the purchase to the CFO, and the one most often run badly.

The discipline is in the chain: objective → outcome → success metric → reference quarter and goal. If any link is vague, the report is not defensible. Push the sponsor to make objectives business statements (“reduce cost to serve”) rather than IT statements (“complete the next upgrade”).

Outcome Insights is the operational half — it tells us which outcomes are off track and recommends the capability or the training that addresses them. Value Reports are the reporting half, and their credibility comes from showing assumptions rather than hiding them.

State the caveat out loud rather than letting someone discover it later: a meaningful Value Report needs at least two years of product data. That is an argument for starting data collection now, even if the first report is modest.

Slide 14 — Product adoption and recommendations

Three connected ideas: see the gap, plan the closure, execute with help.

The gap point is usually the most confronting for clients — most organisations materially under-use what they already own, and that is a renewal risk as much as an efficiency one.

The planning point is that roadmaps are sequenced against business objectives, not product release order. That is what makes them adoptable by the business.

The execution point is the conversion step: a recommendation that never becomes a work item is just a notification. Route them into SPM, CWM, Jira or Azure DevOps during normal planning.

Raise concurrency honestly: it is a real constraint on how many expert engagements can run at once, and scheduling is subject to availability. Plan engagements in advance rather than assuming immediate start.

Slide 23 — 8. Installation and activation — the sequence (1 of 2)

Steps 1–8 are everything up to the point where the instance is scanning.

Two sequencing traps worth calling out. First, Guided Setup steps lock — you cannot jump ahead, and marking a step complete prematurely hides a failure until much later in the sequence. Mark complete only on genuine success.

Second, step 6 has a non-obvious sub-step that blocks most first installations: when the Scan Engine error banner appears, you must follow the sys_update_version link to the Tables page, open the Application Access tab, select Can read and update. Without that, scanning does not start.

Step 8 timing: a first full scan can legitimately take hours on a large instance. Run it overnight in production and watch Scan Status rather than re-triggering it.

Slide 24 — 8. Installation and activation — the sequence (2 of 2)

Steps 9–15 are the connection and value-enablement half.

Step 9 has three data-quality requirements that cause most validation failures: the instance name must match the instance_name system property exactly, the URL must be exactly https://instancename.service-now.com with no www., no trailing slash and no path, and only one instance in the stack may be designated Production.

Step 11 is where impatience is expensive. Every failed validation attempt contributes to the account lockout policy. If validation fails, fix the root cause first — do not retry blind. If the account does lock, remember that two flags must be cleared, locked_out and password_needs_reset; clearing only one is insufficient.

Step 15 is the step people forget. Installing a data collection pack does not collect data — the jobs must be enabled. And objectives and outcomes should be agreed early, because Value Reports need at least two years of product data.

Slide 25 — 9. The first 90 days — Foundations

Foundations is roughly the first 90–120 days and its job is to get the loop running end to end and produce a first credible read-out.

Two things to emphasise. First, the ordering is deliberate: connection and scans before value reporting, because the value layer needs data to accumulate. Second, the milestone that matters most is at week 12 — not “everything installed” but “findings owned, outcomes baselined, roadmap published, reviews in diaries”.

The most commonly skipped item is the last exit criterion: calendarising the reviews. Programs that do not calendarise the cadence quietly stop using the product within two quarters.

Useful framing for the sponsor: by day 90 we should be able to show the health baseline and the first outcome trends, even though a full value report needs considerably more data history.

Slide 26 — Steady state — the repeating cadence

Steady State is a repeating cadence, not a project phase — this slide is the operating contract.

The two meetings that carry the most weight are the monthly operational and health review (which keeps technical work moving) and the quarterly executive review (which keeps sponsorship alive). If only one can be protected, protect the quarterly executive review.
